What do you keep?

B-records has a batch book for broilers and one for layers. Choose one to read its short guide. You can keep both kinds of batches in the same app — later use menu ☰ → New batch and pick the kind.

This is your broiler batch book. You type what happened on the farm each day — feed, deaths, weight. The app calculates feed cost, live birds, FCR, break-even, and whether the batch made a profit or a loss. Your numbers stay on this phone — nothing is uploaded to the internet.

! Things you must understand before you start

  • Use the Today tab every day Open Today, type feed eaten, deaths and (when you weigh) the average weight, and you are done. What you type there goes straight into the Daily Record and the Batch Report — you do not type it twice. You can also pick any other date on that screen to fill in a missed day, fix a mistake, or see everything that happened on that day: feed, deaths, medicine, costs and sales.
  • Bird weight is entered in grams — not kilograms A day-old chick is usually about 40–45 grams. Type 45, not 0.045. When birds reach about one kilogram, type 1000, not 1. Feed is always in kilograms (kg). Money is in Ugandan Shillings (UGX).
  • Your data stays only on this phone — back up before you uninstall What you type is saved automatically on this device only. Android does not let any app show a screen at the moment you press Uninstall, so if you remove B-records without a backup file, your batches can be lost forever. Before you uninstall, change phones, or clear app data: menu ☰ → Download backup (type 2 for everything) and keep the JSON file in Files, WhatsApp, or Google Drive. On the new phone: install the app → ☰ → Restore backup.
  • Only the grey boxes are for you to fill Anything not in a grey box is calculated for you — total chick cost, water needed, live birds at the end of each day, feed cost, net profit, break-even, and more. Those figures update when you change the grey boxes.
  • Library password is optional When you first save a batch to the library, you may set a password or leave it blank. A password encrypts saved batches and is required to unlock or delete them. Leaving it blank still saves batches on this phone, without a lock. If you Cancel the password step, your current work on screen is not cleared. If you set a password and later forget it, contact Boono support for recovery help.
  • Make a backup before you change phones or uninstall The phone will not ask you to back up when you uninstall — that is an Android limit, not a missing feature. You must back up yourself: ☰ → Download backup → type 2 (everything). Keep the file somewhere safe (B-records folder, WhatsApp to yourself, or Drive). On another phone: install B-records → ☰ → Restore backup → choose how to restore (usually 3 = add to library and open, which keeps any batches already on that phone).
  • You can undo mistakes The top bar has Undo and Redo. If you delete a row, the app asks you to confirm first. After a delete you can still use Undo if you change your mind.
  • Zoom and print Pinch the screen with two fingers to zoom in or out. Tap Print in the top bar to print the section you are viewing, or save it as a PDF from the system print screen.

1 How to use the app — step by step

Set the batch up once (Batch Setup and Feed Program), then use Today every day. Your work is saved as you type.

  1. Batch Setup — always start here. Enter the farm and farmer name, house or pen, and the Placement Date (the day the chicks arrived). Enter how many chicks you placed, the cost of one chick, the opening chick weight in grams, and the selling price you expect per bird. Expected harvest date is optional. House length and width are optional; if you fill them, the app shows stocking density (birds per m²). Target final weight and target FCR are optional goals so you can see whether the batch is on track.
  2. Feed Program Turn each phase on or off (Pre-Starter, Starter, Grower, Finisher). Change the start and end day if your own program is different. Type the price per kilogram for every feed you use. Start and end dates fill automatically from the Placement Date. If you leave feed prices empty, the app cannot calculate true feed cost or true profit.
  3. Today — your daily screen. Pick the date (it opens on today), then type feed eaten (kg), birds that died and the average weight (grams) when you weigh. Under More details you can add the cause of death, temperature, humidity and notes. The screen shows the day's results straight away. Below it, What happened on this day lists the day's figures, medicine, costs and sales, with buttons to add a cost, a sale or a medicine for that date.
  4. Daily Record For each day, enter feed consumed in kilograms and any birds that died (and the cause if you know it). Enter average body weight in grams when you weigh the birds — once a week is fine if you do not weigh every day. Temperature and humidity are optional. If temperature is 30°C or higher, the app uses the hot-weather water ratio. From what you type, the app shows water needed, live birds still in the house, cumulative mortality, daily feed cost, and cumulative FCR. Live birds go down when birds die and when you record a sale on the Sales tab with that date (or an earlier date). Weekly summaries and warnings appear above the table when useful.
  5. Medication Log Record vaccines and treatments: date, product, dosage, route, reason, and withdrawal days if needed. If the vaccination option on this start screen stays ticked, the app can add the standard schedule automatically when you first set a Placement Date (only if the log is still empty). You can edit or delete those rows afterwards.
  6. Operational Costs Labour, charcoal, litter, transport, and any other expense for this batch. These are added into total cost and profit on the Batch Report.
  7. Sales When you sell birds (full batch or partial offtake), add a row: date, number of birds, price per bird, and optional notes. Sales increase revenue on the Batch Report and reduce live birds in the Daily Record from that sale date onward. If you never enter sales, the report uses the setup selling price for birds still available.
  8. Batch Report The full picture: mortality, survival, final weight, FCR, all costs, revenue, net profit or loss, and simple trend charts. Use Print in the top bar for a paper or PDF copy, or copy a short summary for WhatsApp. Menu ☰ also has Export to Excel and Export to Word — files go to the main B-records folder on the phone.
  9. Menu ☰ — library, backups, and settings

    Empty template stores your usual defaults only (farm names, prices, feed phases) — not daily data. Tap Update empty template from current when your defaults are set the way you like. Tap Use on the empty template to start a clean batch with those defaults.

    Save batch to library stores the full batch (including daily records), encrypted with your library password. Unlock the library with that password. Deleting a saved batch always asks for the password again.

    Download backup / Restore backup / Export one batch Download backup: type 1 = the batch on screen only; type 2 = everything (screen + library + empty template). File goes into the phone’s B-records folder when storage is allowed.
    Export on a library row saves that one batch only — other batches are not in the file.
    Restore backup: type 1 open on screen only (library unchanged); 2 add into library only (screen unchanged, other batches kept); 3 add to library and open; 4 full replace only if the file is a full backup (overwrites library — use with care).
    Always download a backup before uninstalling the app.

    New batch / Load / Use empty template automatically save the batch on screen into the library first (when there is data to keep), so you do not lose the last numbers you typed. You can still use Save batch to library any time.


    Dark mode and Desktop view change appearance and layout. Open Instructions anytime to read this guide again.

You can open these instructions again at any time from the menu ☰ → Instructions, or from the Instructions link at the bottom of each section.

1 How to use the app — step by step

Set the batch up once, then use Today every day. Your work is saved as you type.

  1. Batch Setup — always start here (one time). Enter the batch name, farm and farmer, house or pen, the breed, the Placement Date (the day the birds arrived), and what you bought (day-old chicks, young pullets or point-of-lay pullets, with their age in weeks for pullets). Enter how many you bought and the cost of one bird. Enter your selling price per tray (and, if you want, per egg) and any other revenue. The app estimates Birds at Lay Onset for the hen-housed %; type your own number if the estimate is off. The Lifecycle Milestones (lay onset, peak, planned depletion) are filled with standard weeks — change them if your plan is different. Water ratios, house size and targets are optional.
  2. Phases & Feed Two tables you can edit. Lifecycle Phases are the names the app shows for each stage (Brooding, Rearing, Growing, Pre-Lay, Lay, Post-Peak, Late-Lay) — change the names or weeks, or add and delete phases. Feed Program has the five feeds: turn each on or off, change the weeks, and type the price per kg of each feed you use. Without feed prices the app cannot calculate true feed cost or profit.
  3. Today — your daily screen. Pick the date (it opens on today), then type feed consumed (kg), eggs collected, broken eggs, deaths and culls. Under More details you can add body weight, temperature, cause of death, egg sizes and notes. The screen shows the day's results straight away: live birds, hen-day %, trays, feed per bird, feed cost. Below it, What happened on this day lists the day's figures, vaccines, medication, costs and sales, with buttons to add a cost, a sale, a medication, or to mark a vaccine as done for that date.
  4. Daily Record The same days as a table, up to 525 days (75 weeks), 4 weeks at a time. Use it to look over many days at once or to correct older days. Rows turn red for high mortality, yellow for heat, and orange for a sudden egg drop.
  5. Weekly Summary Nothing to type here. The app adds up your daily rows for each week: feed, feed cost, water, deaths, culls, eggs, trays, hen-day %, hen-housed %, feed per bird, body weight and temperature.
  6. Health & Vaccines The Vaccination Program is already filled with the standard schedule below; planned dates are calculated from your Placement Date and the age you bought the birds. When you give a vaccine, type the actual date and tap Done (or use Today). Overdue vaccines show in red and vaccines due within 7 days in amber. Vaccines that were due before the birds arrived show “Before placement” — check with your supplier that they were done. The Medication Log below is for any treatment: date, product, dosage, route, reason, and egg withdrawal days.
  7. Operational Costs Labour, litter, egg trays and crates, lighting, transport, repairs and any other expense. Type the money in the Amount (UGX) column. These are added into total cost and profit.
  8. Sales For spent hens, culled birds, manure, feed bags and other sales. Rows for Spent hens or Culled birds also remove those birds from the live birds from the sale date onward. To record your actual egg sales, add rows with the item Egg trays or Loose eggs — then egg revenue uses what you really sold. If you add none, the app assumes every sellable egg is sold at your tray price.
  9. Batch Report The full picture: birds, mortality, trays and eggs, hen-day %, peak, hen-housed %, feed per egg, all costs and revenue, net profit or loss, cost per tray, break-even, and trend charts. Use Print in the top bar, or copy a short summary for WhatsApp. Menu ☰ also has Export to Excel and Export to Word.
  10. Menu ☰ — library, backups, and settings

2 How the numbers are calculated

Every calculated figure follows the layer batch record. Here is what each one means, so you can check it yourself.

FigureHow it is worked out
Live birdsBirds bought − deaths − culls − spent hens / culled birds sold (counted from the sale date). Shown for the end of each day.
Sellable eggsEggs collected − broken / rejected eggs.
TraysSellable eggs ÷ eggs per tray (30 unless you change it).
Hen-day %Eggs collected ÷ live birds that day × 100. It tells you how hard the birds in the house are laying.
Hen-housed %Eggs collected ÷ Birds at Lay Onset × 100. It also counts hens that died, so it is equal to or lower than hen-day %.
Average / Peak hen-dayAverage = all eggs ÷ all bird-days with eggs recorded. Peak = the best week (at least 4 egg days recorded).
Feed per birdFeed kg × 1000 ÷ live birds (grams per bird per day). Layers usually eat about 110–125 g/day at peak.
Feed per eggFeed eaten in lay (grams) ÷ eggs collected.
WaterFeed kg × 2 litres (× 2.5 when the day is 30 °C or more). You can change both ratios in Batch Setup.
Feed costFeed kg × the price per kg of the feed that applies to that week of life.
Total costCost of the birds + feed cost + operational costs.
Egg revenueYour logged egg sales if you added any; otherwise trays × tray price (or sellable eggs × egg price).
Total revenueEgg revenue + spent hen sales + other sales + other revenue.
Net profit / lossTotal revenue − total cost. Green is profit, red is loss.
Cost per egg / trayTotal cost ÷ sellable eggs, and total cost ÷ trays.
Break-even price per tray(Total cost − income that is not from eggs) ÷ trays produced. The lowest tray price that avoids a loss for the trays produced so far.
Break-even trays to sellTotal cost ÷ your tray price, rounded up. How many trays you must sell at your price to cover all your costs so far. Other income is not counted.
